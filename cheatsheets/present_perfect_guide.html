Simple Past & Present Perfect

past tense · te-form + いる · context words
Grammar · Intermediate

The Key Distinction

Concept
How Japanese handles it In Japanese, the affirmative past tense does double duty — it covers both simple past and present perfect. Context words like きのう or もう signal which reading is intended. The difference only becomes grammatically visible in the negative.

まだ〜ていません

Pattern
まだ + 〜て (te-form) + いません = "have not ~ yet"
Breakdown 〜て = te-form of the verb  ·  いません = present negative of いる (iru)  ·  まだ = "not yet" (connects the event to the present)
