あげる / くれる

(Giver) が/は (Recipient) に (Object) を あげる / くれる  ·  "I give…"
Grammar · Intermediate

Two verbs for "give"

Rules
Key Rule Japanese has two verbs for giving: あげる (ageru) and くれる (kureru). Which one you use depends on the direction of the giving, not on who the people are.
Giver+が / は(ga / wa)+Recipient+に(ni)+Object を(o)+あげる / くれる →"Giver gives Recipient (object)"
Particles With both verbs the giver is the subject, marked with は (wa) or が (ga). The recipient is marked with に (ni). The thing given takes を (o).

Direction

あげる vs くれる
VerbWho gives to whomDirection
あげるageru I → you
I → others
You → others
Others → others		 Outward, away from me
"I" → "You" → "Others"
くれるkureru Others → me
Others → you
You → me		 Inward, toward me
"Others" → "You" → "I"
Quick test Is the object travelling away from me (or between two other people)? → あげる (ageru). Is it coming toward me? → くれる (kureru).

Examples

Examples
Example 1 · あげる, present
"I will give Maki flowers."
私(わたし)はwatashi wa
まきMaki
にni
花(はな)をhana o
あげます。agemasu.
Giver: 私 (I) · Recipient: まき (boxed) · あげます = present affirmative of あげる (outward)
Example 2 · あげる, past
"Tomo gave Saki a watch."

さきSaki
にni
時計(とけい)をtokei o
あげました。agemashita.
Giver: とも · Recipient: さき · others give to others → あげる
Example 3 · くれる, short form + かもしれません
"My parents may give me a new car."
両親(りょうしん)はryoushin wa
私(わたし)watashi
にni
車(くるま)をkuruma o
くれるkureru
かもしれません。kamoshiremasen.
Giver: 両親 · Recipient: 私 (me, boxed) · くれる = short form, because かもしれません follows a short form · coming toward me → くれる
Example 4 · くれる, close family member
"The President gave my little sister a letter."
大統領(だいとうりょう)がdaitouryou ga
妹(いもうと)imouto
にni
手紙(てがみ)をtegami o
くれました。kuremashita.
The recipient is not "me", but she is close family, so the speaker feels benefited → くれる

When can くれる be used for someone close?

Key Rule
Benefit rule くれる (kureru) is possible only in limited contexts: when you feel you yourself benefited because somebody very close to you received something.

It should be easy to identify yourself with a member of your immediate family or a very good friend. In Example 4, the little sister (妹 (imouto)) is family, so くれる is used.
