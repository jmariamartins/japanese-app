★ Quick-reference summary of group rules:
Pres. Aff: A (verbs + い-adj) = dict. form · B (な-adj + noun) = desu → da
Pres. Neg: C (adj + noun) = drop desu · D (verbs) = drop -u → add -anai / -nai for ru-verbs
Past Aff: A (verbs) = te-form te/de → ta/da · B (い-adj) = final -i → katta · C (な-adj + noun) = da → datta
Past Neg: B (ALL types) = take pres. neg. short form, replace final -i → katta
Part II
Usages of Short Forms
Usage Structure & Notes Example
(1) Quotation "She said…" / "I think…" short form + to itteimashita → reporting what someone said short form + to omoimasu → expressing your own thoughts ("I think that…") to = quotation particle (like "that" in indirect speech)

★ Tense is preserved: if the original utterance was in the present, the quoted clause must also stay in the present — even if you are reporting it now in the past.

★ To say "I don't think X", prefer:
  [neg. short form] + to omoimasu
  (NOT: to omoimasen) 		"Mary said there would be an exam tomorrow." Mary wa ashita shiken ga aru to itteimashita.
"Sue said she was studying Japanese." (Sue used present tense, so keep it present) Su- wa nihongo o benkyoushiteiru to itteimashita.
"Sue said she had lunch." (past in original → past short form) Su- wa ohirugohan o tabeta to itteimashita.
"I think Takeshi does not like Mary." Takeshi wa Mary ga sukijanai to omoimasu.
(2) Casual Speech Signs of intimacy / informal register · Question particle ka drops → use rising intonation only
· da ending on な-adj and nouns usually drops in questions
· "Yes" = un  ·  "No" = uun
· Past short forms work exactly the same way present / past short form + ? (rising tone) 		Q: "Do you often eat fish?" (present) Sakana o yoku taberu? A: "Yes." / "No." Un. Yoku taberu.  /  Uun. Tabenai.
Q: "Have you eaten dinner?" (past) Yorugohan, tabeta? A: "Yeah, I have." Un, tabeta.
(3) Negative Requests "Please don't do…" negative short form (verb) + de kudasai Used to politely ask someone to refrain from doing something. "Please don't run here." Koko de hashiranai de kudasai.
"Please don't eat here." Koko de tabenai de kudasai.
(4) Expressing Ideas "I like doing…" / "I'm good at…" (person) wa [verb dict. form] no ga + [feeling] Short forms are used where a verb / adjective is treated as a noun.
no ga nominalises the verb (like "-ing" in English).

Feelings (endings): · suki desu — like
· kirai desu — dislike
· jouzu desu — good at (about others)
· heta desu — bad at (about others)
· tokui desu — comfortable with (about yourself)
· nigate desu — uncomfortable with (about yourself) ★ Common mistake: Do NOT use te-form here.
✗ hanashite ga heta desu
✓ hanasu no ga heta desu 		"I like studying Japanese." Nihongo o benkyousuru no ga suki desu.
"Takeshi is bad at speaking English." (about others) Takeshi wa eigo o hanasu no ga heta desu.
"I'm not comfortable with cooking." (about yourself) Ryouri suru no ga nigate desu.
★ Past tense in usages: Past short forms work in all four usages exactly like present short forms — the tense just shifts. In quotation, always preserve the tense of the original utterance inside the と clause.
