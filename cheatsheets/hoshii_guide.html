ほしい

[object] が ほしいです  ·  "I want (a thing)"
Grammar · Intermediate

Formation

Rules
Key Rule Use ほしい (hoshii) to say you want a thing (a noun). The wanted object usually takes が (ga). To say you don't want something, use は (wa) or が (ga) with ほしくない (hoshikunai).
Object+が(ga)+ほしいです。(hoshii desu) →"I want [object]"
Object+は / が(wa / ga)+ほしくないです。(hoshikunai desu) →"I don't want [object]"
ほしい vs たい ほしい (hoshii) goes with a thing (車が ほしい kuruma ga hoshii = I want a car). たい (tai) goes on a verb stem (食べたい tabetai = I want to eat). See the たい guide.

Important: ほしい is for YOUR wants only

Key Rule
Private predicates Like たい (tai), ほしい (hoshii) is primarily limited to the first person. These words are called "private predicates": they refer to inner sensations known only to the person feeling them.

For a second or third person, Japanese needs an extra device. Others' wants are reported by quotation, guess or observation.

Quoting — と言っています

Quotation
Rule Quote the people who say they feel the sensation: short form ほしい + と言っています ("someone says…").
Quotation Example
"Robert says he wants a laptop."
ロバートはRobaato wa
パソコンがpasokon ga
ほしいhoshii
と言(い)っています。to itte imasu.
ほしい = present affirmative short form · と言っています = "someone says…"

Guessing — でしょう

Guess
Rule Make it clear you are only guessing: short form + でしょう ("probably").
Guess Example
"Probably Kyoko does not want a donut."
きょうこはKyouko wa
ドーナツがdoonatsu ga
ほしくないhoshikunai
でしょう。deshou.
ほしくない = present negative short form · でしょう = guess / prediction

Observing — ほしがる

Observation
Rule ほしがる is the special verb counterpart of ほしい (just as たがる replaces たい). It conjugates as a u-verb and is usually used as ほしがっている to describe an observation that you currently think holds true.
Particle note Unlike ほしい, the object of desire takes を with ほしがっている.
