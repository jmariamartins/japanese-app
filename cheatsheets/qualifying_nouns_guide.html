What is Noun Qualification?

Concept
Key Rule

In Japanese, the qualifying phrase always comes before the noun it modifies — just like adjectives. The qualifying phrase can be an adjective, an adjective + subject phrase, or a full verb clause in the short form.

[ Qualifying phrase ] + Noun
Important

The short forms of verbs can qualify nouns, much like adjectives can. This is one of the most important patterns for describing people and things in Japanese.

Word Order Reminder

Japanese is a head-final language — the noun being described always comes last. The entire qualifying clause, no matter how long, sits in front of it. There is no equivalent of "who / which / that" as a separate word; the verb or adjective form alone signals the relative clause.
