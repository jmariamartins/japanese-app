〜そうです

adjective / verb stem + そう  ·  "It looks like…" / "It seems…"
Grammar · Intermediate

Formation

Rules
Key Rule Add そう to an adjective to say something looks a certain way. You are guessing from what you can see or sense, without conclusive evidence.
TypeRuleExample
い-adjectiveDrop the final い and add そう おいしい → おいしそうoishii → oishisou
な-adjectiveDrop the final な and add そう 元気(げんき)な → 元気そうgenki na → genki sou
いいIrregular いい → よさそうii → yosasou

Negative: ない → なさそう

Negative
Watch out You can use そうです (sou desu) with negative adjectives too. The negative ending ない (nai) changes to なさ (nasa) before そう (sou).

Examples

Examples
Example 1 · い-adjective
"This apple looks delicious."
このりんごはkono ringo wa
おいしそうです。oishisou desu.
おいしい → remove い, add そう = おいしそう → conjugate to present affirmative: そうです
Example 2 · いい
"It looks like the weather will be fine tomorrow."
明日(あした)は天気(てんき)がashita wa tenki ga
良(よ)さそうです。yosasou desu.
いい → よさそう (irregular)
Example 3 · な-adjective, past
"It looked like Mary was fine."
メアリーはMearii wa
元気(げんき)そうでした。genki sou deshita.
元気な → remove な, add そう → past: そうでした
Example 4 · negative い-adjective
"This book does not look difficult."
この本(ほん)はkono hon wa
難(むずか)しくなさそうです。muzukashiku nasasou desu.
難しくない → ない becomes なさ + そう → そうです
Example 5 · negative な-adjective
"It does not look like Tomoko is good at tennis."
ともこはテニスがTomoko wa tenisu ga
上手(じょうず)じゃなさそうです。jouzu ja nasasou desu.
上手じゃない → じゃなさそう → そうです

Before a noun: そうな

Qualifier
Rule You can use adjective + そう (sou) to qualify a noun. そう (sou) is a な-adjective, so it becomes そうな (sou na) before a noun.
Example · noun qualifier
"She wears a warm-looking sweater."
暖(あたた)かそうなatatakasou na
セーターをseetaa o
着(き)ています。kite imasu.
そうな stays そうな because it qualifies a noun

When to use it

Evidence
Visual impressions In many そうです (sou desu) sentences the guess is based on what you see. But そうな (sou na) is not tied only to sight. We use it whenever we lack conclusive evidence.
Before vs after An apple is おいしそう (oishisou) before you have tasted it. Once you have tasted it, you say おいしい (oishii). Likewise, if the evidence is already enough to conclude something (for example you can see that something is pretty), you simply say きれいです (kirei desu) rather than きれいそうです (kirei sou desu).

Verb stem + そうです

Imminent
Rule Attach そうです (sou desu) to a verb stem (the form before ます (masu)) to describe an imminent event: it looks like it is about to happen.
Example · imminent event
"It looks like it will rain."
雨(あめ)がame ga
ふりそうです。furisou desu.
ふり (stem of ふる) + そうです
Irregular negative The negative of verb stem + そうです (sou desu) is somewhat irregular: 〜そうもありません (~sou mo arimasen).
Example · negative
"It does not look like it will rain."
雨(あめ)がame ga
ふりそうもありません。furisou mo arimasen.
ふり + そうもありません
Extra · not the same そうです 〜そうです (~sou desu) after a short form means "I heard that…" (hearsay), e.g. 雨が降るそうです (ame ga furu sou desu) = "I hear it will rain." The "looks like" そうです (sou desu) in this guide attaches to the adjective stem or verb stem instead.
